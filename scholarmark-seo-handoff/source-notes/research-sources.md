# Research Sources

Use these as evidence links for public content, internal briefs, and implementation notes.

## Student AI Use And Risk

- [HEPI Student Generative AI Survey 2025](https://www.hepi.ac.uk/reports/student-generative-ai-survey-2025/)
  - Useful stats: 92% of surveyed students reported using any AI tool; 51% cited false results or hallucinations as a factor putting them off AI use.
  - Use for: market urgency, student adoption, trust gap.

- [Scientific Reports: Fabrication and errors in the bibliographic citations generated by ChatGPT](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-41032-5)
  - Useful stats: in tested outputs, 55% of GPT-3.5 citations and 18% of GPT-4 citations were fabricated.
  - Use for: citation verification pages, hallucinated citation warnings.

- [Nature: Hallucinated citations are polluting the scientific literature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-00969-z)
  - Use for: current context that fabricated references are a broad research-integrity issue.

## Academic Integrity And Writing Center Guidance

- [Cornell Center for Teaching Innovation: AI and Academic Integrity](https://teaching.cornell.edu/generative-artificial-intelligence/ai-academic-integrity)
  - Useful point: instructors are advised to require students to verify citations and references.

- [Clemson Writing Lab: AI in Academic Writing](https://www.clemson.edu/centers-institutes/writing/writing-resources/writing-resources/ai-in-academic-writing.html)
  - Useful point: verify AI-generated content with reliable sources and double-check citations.

- [USC Academic Integrity and Generative AI](https://academicintegrity.usc.edu/faculty-resources/addressing-generative-ai/)
  - Useful point: verify citations and quotes because AI tools can generate fictional sources or misattribute material.

- [UC Davis Writing Center: Writing with AI Quick Guide](https://writingcenter.ucdavis.edu/writing-resources/writing-with-AI-quick-guide)
  - Useful point: keep a record of generative AI work and follow professor guidance.

- [UNC Writing Center: Generative AI in Academic Writing](https://writingcenter.unc.edu/tips-and-tools/generative-ai-in-academic-writing/)
  - Useful point: AI citations and quotes may be invented; students remain responsible for submitted work.

## RAG And Source Grounding

- [Stanford/Wiley: Hallucination-Free? Assessing the Reliability of Leading AI Legal Research Tools](https://dho.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/Legal_RAG_Hallucinations.pdf)
  - Useful point: RAG-based tools can reduce hallucinations relative to general chatbots, but the problem persists. Use to avoid "no hallucinations" claims.

- [Google NotebookLM Help: Use chat in NotebookLM](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16179559?hl=en)
  - Useful point: NotebookLM uses source citations and tells users to double-check because it can make mistakes.

- [AWS: What is Retrieval-Augmented Generation?](https://aws.amazon.com/what-is/retrieval-augmented-generation/)
  - Use for: plain RAG definitions if needed.

- [Google Cloud: Retrieval-Augmented Generation](https://cloud.google.com/use-cases/retrieval-augmented-generation)
  - Use for: RAG explainer support.
