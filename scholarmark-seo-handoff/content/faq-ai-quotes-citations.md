## What is a hallucinated quote?

A hallucinated quote is a quotation that an AI tool presents as real even though the exact wording cannot be found in the source. Sometimes the source exists but the quote does not. Sometimes both the quote and source are invented.

## What is a hallucinated citation?

A hallucinated citation is a reference that looks scholarly but does not match a real source, or points to a real source that does not support the claim. It may include realistic author names, titles, journal names, dates, or DOIs.

## Why do AI tools invent citations?

Large language models generate likely text patterns. If asked for sources, they may produce text that looks like a citation even when they have not verified it against a real database or source.

## Can AI invent quotes from real sources?

Yes. AI can produce a quote-like sentence that sounds plausible for a real author or article without matching the original text. That is why exact quote verification matters, not just source lookup.

## How can I check whether an AI citation is real?

Search for the exact title, DOI, author, and journal in your library database, Google Scholar, Crossref, the publisher site, or your course-approved research database. Then check that the source actually supports the claim you want to make.

## How do I check whether a quote is real?

Open the original source and search for the exact wording. If the document is scanned or OCR quality is poor, check the likely page or section manually. Do not use quotation marks unless the wording is exact.

## How does source context help?

Source context helps you see whether the quote means what you think it means. The surrounding paragraph may qualify, narrow, or even reverse the meaning of a sentence that looks useful in isolation.

## Can RAG or source-grounded AI eliminate hallucinations?

No responsible tool should promise that. Source grounding can reduce risk by making source material visible and checkable, but students still need to verify final quotes, citations, paraphrases, and claims.

## What is the safest workflow for using AI in research writing?

Build your source base first. Save exact passages with context. Draft from selected evidence. Verify quotes, citations, and claims before submitting.
