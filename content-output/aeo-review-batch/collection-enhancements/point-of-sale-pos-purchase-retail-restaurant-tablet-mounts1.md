## Use-Case Section Copy

### POS Tablet Mounts for Restaurants, Retail Counters, and Delivery App Workflows

A modern restaurant counter can fill up fast: Toast or Square at the register, DoorDash on one tablet, Uber Eats on another, Grubhub on a third, and a kitchen screen waiting for attention. Retail counters have the same problem in a different form, with mobile checkout, loyalty apps, inventory lookup, and customer-facing payment screens competing for space.

iBOLT POS and restaurant tablet mounts are built for those real workflows. The Tablet Tower lineup organizes multiple tablets vertically, giving staff one predictable place to check every platform. Wall-mounted versions move tablets off the counter entirely, which helps kitchens, expo stations, ghost kitchens, and pickup shelves. For a single checkout tablet, LockPro and Dock'n Lock drill-base stands add a more secure, fixed position.

This collection should speak to operators who are trying to reduce clutter and mistakes, not just buy a stand. The practical value is faster order visibility, fewer tablets sliding around the counter, and a cleaner device station that new staff can understand immediately.

Use this category for restaurants, food trucks, ghost kitchens, retail counters, trade show checkout stations, curbside pickup desks, and mobile POS areas where tablets are used all day.

## FAQ Block

### What is the best tablet mount for restaurants with multiple delivery apps?

A multi-tablet tower is usually the cleanest setup because it organizes several delivery app tablets in one station. The Quad Tablet Tower is a strong fit for restaurants running up to four tablets.

### Should restaurants use a counter stand or wall mount?

Use a counter stand when staff need the tablets near the register or order station. Use a wall mount when counter space is tight or when the tablets belong near the kitchen pass, prep area, or pickup shelf.

### Do POS tablet mounts need to lock?

Locking mounts are recommended for customer-facing tablets, unattended counters, trade shows, kiosks, and shared environments. For back-of-house delivery app tablets, locking may be less important than visibility and organization.

### Can one POS station hold both phones and tablets?

Yes. The collection includes dual-device options and modular parts that can support phone and tablet workflows, depending on the install surface and device sizes.

### What tablets do restaurant POS mounts support?

The product pages should be reviewed for exact size ranges, but the collection is built around common restaurant tablets used with platforms such as Toast, Square, Clover, Shopify POS, DoorDash, Uber Eats, and Grubhub.
