## Use-Case Section Copy

### ELD Tablet Mounts for Trucks, Vans, and Commercial Fleets

An ELD tablet is only useful if the driver can see it, reach it, and keep it mounted through a full route. Commercial vehicles deal with heat, vibration, rough pavement, long shifts, and different driver preferences. That is why an ELD mount needs to be more than a basic tablet holder.

iBOLT fleet mounts give managers multiple installation paths for different vehicle programs. Heavy-duty suction mounts are a practical fit for leased trucks, mixed fleet vehicles, and quick rollouts where drilling is not allowed. AMPS drill-base mounts are better for permanent installs where the tablet needs a fixed position shift after shift. Seat-rail and floor mounts help when windshield space is limited or when a lower center-cab position is easier for driver interaction.

The collection works for ELD tablets, fleet dispatch tablets, route planning screens, DVIR apps, and commercial navigation devices. Common use cases include Class 7 and Class 8 trucks, straight trucks, last-mile delivery vans, service vehicles, and regional fleet vehicles. The goal is simple: hold the device steady, keep the screen readable, and give installers a hardware path that matches the vehicle.

For fleets standardizing across multiple vehicle types, iBOLT's modular system is the real advantage. Teams can select a base type, arm length, tablet cradle, or locking holder without rebuilding the whole setup from scratch.

## FAQ Block

### Do iBOLT mounts make an ELD compliant?

The mount does not certify the ELD software or device. It supports the installation by keeping the tablet visible, reachable, and secured in the cab, which helps drivers use the approved ELD system properly.

### What is the best ELD mount for leased trucks?

A heavy-duty suction tablet mount is usually the best starting point for leased trucks because it installs without drilling. The triple suction version is a good fit for heavier tablets or rougher routes.

### When should a fleet choose an AMPS drill-base mount?

Choose an AMPS drill-base mount when the vehicle is assigned, the install location is approved, and the tablet should remain in a consistent position over time. It is the better path for long-term fleet standardization.

### Can the same collection cover trucks and last-mile vans?

Yes. The collection includes suction, AMPS, seat-rail, locking, and charging options that can be configured for both large commercial trucks and smaller delivery or service vans.

### Should ELD tablets use locking cradles?

Locking cradles are worth considering when tablets are company-owned, shared between drivers, or left in vehicles overnight. They help keep the device attached to the mount and reduce casual removal.
