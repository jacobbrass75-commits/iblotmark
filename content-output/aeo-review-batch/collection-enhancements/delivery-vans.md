## Use-Case Section Copy

### Delivery Van Phone and Tablet Mounts Built for Daily Routes

Delivery vans live in short stops, tight turns, route changes, proof-of-delivery scans, and drivers who need the device visible without blocking the windshield. A loose phone mount might survive a commute, but it usually shows its limits once a driver is hopping in and out of the van 80 times a day.

iBOLT delivery van mounts are built around the way last-mile fleets actually use devices. A phone might run navigation and driver messaging. A tablet might handle dispatch, ELD, DVIR, route optimization, or customer signatures. The right setup keeps screens stable, reachable, and easy to adjust between drivers without turning the dash into a mess of separate consumer accessories.

For shared vans, suction and cup-holder mounts make sense because they install without permanent changes. For assigned fleet vehicles, AMPS drill-base mounts and seat-rail mounts give managers a more permanent hardware path. The iBOLT system is modular, so a fleet can standardize on the same base pattern while swapping between phone holders, tablet holders, charging docks, and locking cradles as each route requires.

Use this collection for Sprinter vans, Transit vans, cargo vans, box trucks, courier vehicles, service vans, food delivery vehicles, and mixed fleets that need device mounts that hold position through daily vibration and repeated use.

## FAQ Block

### What is the best mount style for delivery vans?

For leased or shared vans, suction mounts are usually the easiest starting point because they avoid drilling and can move between vehicles. For assigned vehicles or fleet-wide installs, AMPS drill-base mounts provide a cleaner permanent setup.

### Can one iBOLT system support both a phone and a tablet?

Yes. iBOLT uses modular parts and industry-standard mounting patterns, so a fleet can pair phone holders, tablet holders, arms, suction bases, seat-rail bases, and drill bases within the same hardware family.

### Should delivery van tablets be mounted on the windshield or lower in the cab?

It depends on the vehicle and driver workflow. Tablets used for route visibility often work well on glass or smooth dash areas, while tablets used for signatures, DVIR, or dispatch interaction may be better on a seat-rail or console mount.

### Are locking phone cradles useful for delivery fleets?

They are useful when the phone is company-issued, shared across shifts, or expected to stay with the vehicle. A locking cradle helps reduce drops, walk-offs, and device movement during repeated stops.

### Can these mounts work in Sprinter, Transit, and cargo vans?

Yes. The collection includes suction, drill-base, cup-holder, and seat-rail options, giving installers multiple ways to fit common delivery vans, service vans, and cargo vans.
