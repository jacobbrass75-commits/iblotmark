Restaurant Tablet Hell: How to Stop Missing DoorDash, Uber Eats, and Grubhub Orders

It is 6:45 on a Friday. Your DoorDash tablet is face-down next to the printer. The Uber Eats tablet went to sleep twenty minutes ago because someone bumped the screen timeout setting. The Grubhub tablet is plugged in but the cable is half-out and the battery is at 4 percent. Your expo lead is calling tickets. Nobody is watching the screens. Three orders just came in. Nobody knows.

This is tablet hell, and it happens in kitchens of every size, from single-location diners to ghost kitchen operators running four virtual brands off one prep line.

Quick Answer

Missed third-party delivery orders are almost always a station-design problem, not a staff problem. When tablets are flat on a counter, buried under supplies, or sharing a single cord with three other devices, they get ignored or die. Mounting each tablet vertically at eye level, keeping it powered, and assigning it a fixed position on the line turns a reactive scramble into a manageable workflow. The right restaurant tablet mount setup does not support zero missed orders, but it removes the most common physical reasons orders go unseen.

Why Tablets Keep Getting Ignored During Rush

Third-party platforms depend on the tablet staying active and attended. DoorDash uses the merchant tablet for real-time order and store management tasks, which means the screen needs to be in someone's line of sight at all times. Uber Eats notes that its Orders app needs to stay visible in the foreground and can pause accepting orders after missed orders or offline time. Grubhub's tablet setup guidance similarly assumes the device is in a stable, attended position.

None of that works when the tablet is lying flat on a cluttered counter. The physical setup is the first thing to fix.

Here are the most common reasons tablets fail during service:

  • Tablet is flat on the counter and the screen is not visible from the line
  • Charging cable is loose, short, or shared with other devices
  • Screen timeout is set too short and nobody resets it during rush
  • No assigned owner, so everyone assumes someone else is watching it
  • Multiple tablets are stacked or scattered with no clear organization
  • Tablet slides or gets knocked over when the counter gets busy

The Station-Design Fix: Vertical, Powered, Assigned

The solution is to treat your tablet station the same way you treat your ticket rail. Every position has a purpose. Every device has a home. Nothing moves unless someone deliberately moves it.

That means mounting tablets vertically so the screen faces the person responsible for it, securing the power cable so it cannot be accidentally pulled, and organizing multiple tablets so each platform has its own dedicated screen with no overlap or confusion.

Setting Up the Station: A Pre-Service Checklist

Once the mounts are in place, run through this checklist at the start of every shift. Post it near the tablet station so it becomes part of your opening routine.

  • Each tablet is seated fully in its mount and the locking mechanism is engaged
  • Every tablet is plugged in and showing a charging indicator
  • Each platform app is open and showing the correct store status (open, accepting orders)
  • Screen timeout is set to the longest available interval or disabled for service hours
  • One staff member per station is assigned as the tablet owner for that shift
  • Cables are routed away from the prep area and secured so they cannot be pulled loose
  • Tablet screens are visible from the primary working position at that station

One More Thing: Assign Ownership

The best mount in the world does not help if nobody is responsible for the screen. Physical setup and staff assignment work together. Once the tablets are mounted, vertical, and powered, the last step is making sure one person per station knows those screens are their responsibility during service. That is a management decision, but the mount makes it easier to enforce because the tablet has a home and everyone can see when it is unattended.
