Restaurant Tablet Mounts by Station: Counter, Expo, Kitchen, Pickup, and Food Truck

Friday at 6:45 PM. Your front counter tablet is handling Square POS, your expo shelf has a DoorDash tablet propped against a bus tub, and your kitchen line has a phone taped to the hood. Every station has a different problem: splash, heat, cable reach, customer access, staff sightlines. A mount that solves one station often creates a new problem at the next.

Quick answer: Match the mount type to the station's physical constraints. Counter stations need stability and customer-facing flexibility. Expo and pickup need multi-tablet organization. Kitchen needs heat tolerance and hands-free visibility. Food trucks need compact, secure, vibration-resistant setups. The sections below walk through each one.

Why One Mount Does Not Cover Every Station

Third-party delivery platforms each require their own tablet. The DoorDash Merchant Tablet Learning Center and the Uber Eats Orders app both run on dedicated devices, and Grubhub tablet setup adds a third. Stack those with your POS and a kitchen display and you can easily have four or five tablets across two or three stations.

Square and Toast-style stands are well-built for their own POS terminals. They handle tilt, swivel, and customer-facing rotation cleanly. Where they fall short is flexibility across multiple third-party tablets at different stations. Bouncepad and Compulocks-style enclosures are strong for public-facing kiosks where theft is the primary concern, but the locked enclosure slows staff access when orders are moving fast. iBOLT fits the gap when a restaurant needs flexible placement, multiple tablets at one station, and the option to mount on a counter, clamp to a shelf edge, or fix to a wall without buying a separate system for each location.

Kitchen Line and Food Truck: Heat, Splash, and Small Footprints

The kitchen line has constraints that no other station shares. Heat from the line, steam from the pass, and the need for completely hands-free visibility make wall mounting the practical choice. Toast's Kitchen Display System is purpose-built for back-of-house ticket management, and if you are running Toast KDS hardware, that system handles the kitchen screen well. Where iBOLT adds value is when you need a secondary tablet on the line for a delivery platform or a prep station that is not covered by the main KDS.

The wall mount tower keeps tablets above the splash zone, routes cables cleanly along the pole, and frees every inch of prep surface. For food trucks, the clamp mount version of the four-tablet tower works on a narrow counter edge, and the drill-base option anchors for the long term to the truck's built-in counter so the setup does not shift during transit.
