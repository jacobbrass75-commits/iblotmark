Restaurant Tablet Hell: How to Stop Missing DoorDash, Uber Eats, and Grubhub Orders

It is 6:45 on a Friday. The printer is screaming, the expo line is backed up, and somewhere under a stack of ticket paper, a DoorDash tablet is buzzing with three unacknowledged orders. By the time anyone finds it, two of those orders have auto-cancelled. The driver is already calling the restaurant. The customer is already leaving a one-star review.

If that scenario sounds familiar, you are not dealing with a technology problem. You are dealing with a station-design problem. The tablets are not visible, not powered, and not assigned to anyone. The fix is not a new app setting. It is a physical mounting setup that keeps every order screen in the line of sight of whoever is running expo.

Quick Answer

Mount every third-party tablet at eye level, in a fixed position, with a dedicated power cable. A vertical multi-tablet tower or a drill-base locking stand eliminates the buried-tablet problem by making each screen a permanent part of the station rather than a loose device that migrates to the back shelf. Why Tablets Go Dark During Rush

Third-party delivery platforms depend on the tablet staying active and connected. Uber Eats notes that its Orders app needs to stay visible in the foreground and can pause or go offline after missed orders or idle time. DoorDash uses the merchant tablet for real-time order and store management tasks, which means any interruption in tablet visibility is an interruption in order flow. Grubhub's tablet setup documentation reinforces the same principle: the device needs to be on, charged, and attended.

The four most common reasons tablets fail during service are:

  • The tablet is lying flat on a counter and the screen dims or locks
  • The charging cable gets knocked loose and the battery dies mid-shift
  • The tablet gets physically moved or buried under other items
  • No single staff member owns the tablet, so nobody notices when it goes quiet

All four of those problems are solved by mounting the tablet in a fixed, upright, powered position at the expo station.

Station Design: The Visibility Rule

The expo lead or the person calling tickets needs to see every active order screen without turning around or moving. That means tablets need to be vertical, at counter height or slightly above, and grouped together so a single glance covers all three platforms.

A loose tablet propped against a condiment rack does not meet that standard. A tablet bolted to the counter at a fixed angle does. The difference between those two setups is the difference between catching an order in the first thirty seconds and missing it entirely.

For ghost kitchens running four or more virtual brands, the problem compounds. Each brand may have its own tablet for each platform, which means six to eight devices competing for counter space. A tower format solves the footprint problem without sacrificing visibility.

Implementation Checklist for Your Expo Station

Before your next service, run through this checklist to audit your current tablet setup.

  • Every delivery tablet is mounted vertically and visible from the expo position without moving
  • Every tablet has a dedicated charging cable that is routed and secured so it cannot be knocked loose
  • Each tablet is assigned to a specific staff role, not left as a shared device with no owner
  • Screen timeout is disabled or set to the maximum allowed interval on every delivery tablet
  • The delivery tablet station is physically separated from the POS station to reduce clutter and confusion
  • All tablets are tested at the start of each shift to confirm they are online and accepting orders
