Construction Truck Cab Mounts by Cab Type

It is 6:45 a.m. and your foreman is pulling out of the yard in a crew cab loaded with three phones, a tablet running the ELD, and a GPS unit wedged against the windshield with a suction cup that gave up somewhere around mile 40 yesterday. Meanwhile, the dump truck driver is navigating a tight residential street with his phone sitting loose on the seat. Neither setup is working. The problem is not the devices. It is that every cab type creates a different mounting problem, and a one-size solution rarely fits all of them.

Quick answer: Match your mount to your cab. Regular cabs do well with cup-holder or wedge mounts. Crew cabs and vans benefit from seat-rail or drill-base AMPS mounts. Service bodies and dump trucks often need a fixed drill-base or rail solution. The sections below break it down by cab type.

Why Cab Type Changes Everything

A standard pickup cab has a narrow console, limited dash real estate, and one cup holder that is already occupied by coffee. A crew cab adds rear-seat passengers, longer center consoles, and sometimes a second device for a co-pilot or inspector. A service body truck, as defined by vocational body builders like Knapheide and Monroe Truck Equipment, often has a stripped or upfitted cab interior where factory dash trim is already modified. Dump trucks and vans present their own geometry entirely.

The FMCSA requires that a portable ELD be mounted in a fixed position and visible to the driver from the normal seated position. That rule alone eliminates the loose-on-the-seat approach for any commercial vehicle running hours-of-service logs. And the FMCSA mobile phone restrictions reinforce that hands-free, close-proximity placement is the standard for commercial drivers. Better mounting improves visibility and access. It does not support compliance on its own, but it removes the reaching and fumbling that creates risk.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a suction mount in a dump truck or service body cab?

Suction mounts work on flat glass surfaces but are not ideal for high-vibration cabs. A drill-base AMPS mount or seat-rail mount holds more reliably in those environments.

Does FMCSA require a specific mount type for ELD tablets?

FMCSA requires the ELD to be mounted in a fixed position and visible from the normal seated position. The mount type is not specified, but it must stay fixed while driving.

What is the advantage of AMPS compatibility across a mixed fleet?

AMPS is an industry-standard four-hole pattern, so one head unit can move between different bases in different vehicles without buying new hardware for each cab type.

Are vehicle-specific mounts like ProClip a better choice than modular mounts?

Vehicle-specific mounts offer a clean, no-drill fit for a single truck model. Modular AMPS-based systems are more practical when your fleet includes multiple cab types or changes vehicles regularly.
