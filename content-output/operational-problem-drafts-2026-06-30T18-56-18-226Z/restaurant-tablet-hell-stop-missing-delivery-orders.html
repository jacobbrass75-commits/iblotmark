Restaurant Tablet Hell: How to Stop Missing DoorDash, Uber Eats, and Grubhub Orders

It is Friday at 6:45 PM. The printer is screaming, the line is four deep, and somewhere behind the expo station a DoorDash tablet is face-down on a cutting board, screen dark, battery at 11 percent. Nobody noticed. The order sat for nine minutes before the platform auto-cancelled it. That is a refund, a potential account ding, and a customer who will not reorder.

This is not a staffing problem. It is a station-design problem. When tablets are not mounted, they get moved, muted, unplugged, and ignored. The fix is treating every third-party tablet like a piece of kitchen equipment: assigned a permanent spot, always powered, always visible.

Quick Answer

Mount every active delivery tablet at eye level in a fixed, powered position at the expo or make station. Use a multi-tablet tower for high-volume operations running three or more platforms, and a single drill-base stand for counters where space is tight. Keep every screen on, charged, and facing the person responsible for accepting orders.

Why Tablets Keep Getting Lost During Rush

Third-party platforms depend on the tablet staying active and connected. Uber Eats notes that its Orders app needs to stay visible in the foreground and can pause order acceptance after missed orders or offline time. DoorDash explains that the merchant tablet handles order and store management tasks in real time, meaning any interruption in tablet availability is an interruption in order flow. Grubhub's tablet setup guidance similarly assumes the device is always on and accessible.

None of that works when the tablet is lying flat on a shelf, buried under a ticket roll, or charging in a back corner because someone needed counter space. The platform does not know your rush is bad. It only knows the tablet went quiet.

Common causes of tablet chaos during service:

  • No fixed mount, so the tablet migrates to wherever there is open counter space
  • Charging cable too short to reach a mounted position, so the tablet sits near the outlet instead
  • Multiple platforms on separate tablets with no clear ownership or visual priority
  • Screen timeout set too aggressively, causing the app to drop to the background
  • Loud kitchen environment where audio alerts go unheard and no one is watching the screen

The Station-Design Fix: Vertical, Visible, Powered

The goal is a tablet station where every screen is vertical, at eye level, and connected to power at all times. Staff should be able to glance at the station and immediately see which platform has an incoming order without touching anything.

For operations running DoorDash, Uber Eats, and Grubhub simultaneously, that means three tablets minimum, often four if you include a POS or kitchen display. A flat counter cannot hold that many screens in a usable way. A vertical tower can.

Station Setup Checklist Before Your Next Rush

  • Every active delivery tablet is mounted vertically at eye level
  • Every tablet is plugged into power at the mount location, not at a separate outlet
  • Screen timeout is disabled or set to never on each delivery device
  • Each platform app is pinned to the foreground on its assigned tablet
  • One staff member per shift is assigned ownership of the tablet station
  • No loose tablets or phones are allowed on the expo counter during service
  • Cable management is clean enough that no one trips over or unplugs a charging cable by accident

Better mounting improves visibility, access, placement, and charging discipline. It does not support every order gets accepted, but it removes the physical reasons orders get missed in the first place.
