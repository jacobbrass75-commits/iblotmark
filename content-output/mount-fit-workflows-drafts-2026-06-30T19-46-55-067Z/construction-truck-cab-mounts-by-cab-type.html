Construction Truck Cab Mounts by Cab Type

It is 6:45 a.m. and your crew is pulling out of the yard. One driver is in a regular-cab dump truck with no center console and nowhere obvious to put a phone. Another is in a crew-cab service truck with a full console but a dash that slopes at an angle nothing sticks to. A third is in a Sprinter-style cargo van with a flat dash and a cup holder that wobbles. Same fleet, three completely different mounting problems.

The fix is not one universal mount. It is matching the mount type to the cab type. Here is how to do that.

Quick Answer

Regular cabs and dump trucks do best with a drill-base AMPS mount or a wedge. Crew cabs and service trucks work well with a cup-holder mount or a wedge. Cargo vans and Sprinters are ideal for a drill-base pedestal. Rail-equipped cabs and utility trucks benefit from a clamp-style rail mount. Match the base to the surface you actually have, not the surface you wish you had.

Why Cab Type Changes Everything

A regular pickup cab often has a narrow dash, no center console, and limited flat real estate. A crew cab adds console space but also adds rear-seat passengers who bump and shift things. A service body or chassis-cab truck, like those in the Ford Super Duty Chassis Cab, Chevrolet Silverado HD Chassis Cab, or Ram Chassis Cab lineup, often gets upfitted with custom bodies that change the interior layout entirely. Cargo vans have flat dashes but vibrate heavily on highway runs. Each surface type calls for a different anchor strategy.

OSHA notes that reaching for a device is one of the primary distracted-driving risks in work vehicles. Placement matters as much as the mount itself. A phone that requires a driver to look down or reach across the cab creates the exact problem a mount is supposed to solve.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use the same phone head unit across different mounts in a mixed fleet?

Yes, if the mounts share a common interface. iBOLT's AMPS-compatible bases let you move the same head unit between a drill-base pedestal, a wedge, and a cup-holder base without buying a new phone holder each time.

Is a cup-holder mount stable enough for rough jobsite roads?

A quality cup-holder mount with a locking or friction-fit base handles most paved and gravel jobsite roads well. For sustained off-road or heavy-vibration routes, a drill-base mount gives more consistent stability.

What is the best mount for a dump truck with an older cab and minimal dash space?

A drill-base AMPS mount on a flat dash panel or console box is the most reliable option. It does not depend on a cup holder or a console gap that older cabs often lack.

Do I need to drill to use the Bizmount Wedge or the cup-holder mount?

No. Both the xProDock Bizmount Wedge and the xProDock Bizmount Console are tool-free installs with no drilling or adhesives required.
