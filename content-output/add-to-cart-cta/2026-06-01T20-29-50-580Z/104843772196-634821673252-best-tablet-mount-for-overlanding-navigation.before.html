What overlanding tablet mounting must handle

Start with the environment. Trail vibration, device weight with protective cases, dashboard space constraints, and passenger access all determine whether a mount will actually work. A 10-inch iPad in an OtterBox weighs significantly more than the naked device, and that weight matters when bouncing through ruts at 15 mph.

Dashboard real estate is limited in most overlanding vehicles. The mount needs to position the screen where both driver and navigator can see it without blocking gauges, switches, or sightlines. Cable routing for charging becomes critical on multi-day trips where the tablet runs navigation apps for 8-12 hours daily.

Screen orientation flexibility is crucial. Navigation apps work better in landscape mode for most trail use, but some mapping functions prefer portrait orientation. The 360-degree rotation in iBOLT TabDock mounts handles both orientations without removing the tablet from the holder.

Installation planning and verification

Before ordering, confirm your mounting surface. Suction mounts require smooth, non-porous surfaces free of texture or coating. Wedge mounts need adequate console gap width and depth. Drill-base mounts require access to the back side of the mounting surface for hardware installation.

Test the mounting position with a cardboard mockup before final installation. Sit in both driver and passenger seats to verify screen visibility, reach, and interference with normal vehicle operation. The mount should position the screen within easy reach but not block critical controls or sightlines.

Consider removal frequency. If the tablet needs to come out nightly for security or charging, choose hardware that makes this convenient. If the tablet stays mounted for weeks at a time, prioritize retention strength over quick-release features.
