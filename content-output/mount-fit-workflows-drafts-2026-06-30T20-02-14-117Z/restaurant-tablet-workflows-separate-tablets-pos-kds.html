Restaurant Tablet Workflows: Separate Tablets, POS Integration, KDS, and Pickup Screens

It is 12:15 on a Friday. Three delivery orders just came in on separate tablets, the kitchen display is buried under a stack of printed tickets, and your expo person is spinning between the counter and the pass. The software is fine. The physical setup is the problem.

Most restaurant operators have been through some version of this. The good news is that the workflow decisions and the mounting decisions are both solvable, and they are more connected than most people realize.

Quick Answer

A well-designed restaurant tablet setup matches each operational role, ordering, kitchen display, pickup confirmation, delivery acceptance, and customer-facing checkout, to the right software approach and the right physical position. Aggregator software can reduce tablet count, but most restaurants still need multiple screens in specific locations. Mounting those screens correctly is what keeps the workflow intact under pressure.

Before choosing hardware or mounts, it helps to understand how each workflow model actually operates in a restaurant environment.

Where Physical Mounting Still Matters

Aggregator software is genuinely useful. Consolidating three platform tablets into one screen reduces clutter and simplifies staff training. But it does not eliminate the need for physical screens in specific locations. Here is where mounting remains critical regardless of software approach.

Kitchen Display Systems (KDS): Even when the front-of-house runs on a single POS-integrated tablet, the kitchen still needs a dedicated display. Toast's Kitchen Display System is a strong example of purpose-built KDS hardware for back-of-house workflows. Whether you use a proprietary KDS or a tablet running KDS software, that screen needs a fixed, readable position at the cook line or expo pass.

Pickup and Expo Screens: Customers and runners need to see order status. A tablet mounted at the pickup counter, showing order numbers or names, reduces staff interruptions and speeds handoff. This screen is separate from the ordering workflow entirely.

Delivery Acceptance: Even with aggregator software, many operators keep a dedicated screen at the host stand or counter for delivery driver check-ins. That screen needs to be visible to staff and drivers simultaneously, which means positioning matters as much as software.

Backup Workflows: When a POS goes down or an aggregator app loses connectivity, operators fall back to individual platform tablets. Having those tablets mounted and charged means the backup is actually usable.

Station Setup Decision Checklist

  • Identify every screen role: ordering, KDS, pickup display, delivery acceptance, customer kiosk, backup
  • Decide whether aggregator software consolidates your ordering tablets or whether you run separate platform tablets
  • Confirm KDS placement at the cook line or expo pass regardless of front-of-house software
  • Choose locking mounts for any public-facing or high-traffic tablet
  • Use multi-tablet mounts at delivery stations where more than two screens need to be visible simultaneously
  • Plan for backup tablet access: mounted, charged, and ready even if not in active use
  • Match mount type to surface: wall mount where counter space is limited, weighted base where drilling is not an option, drill base where permanent security is the priority

Frequently Asked Questions

Does aggregator software eliminate the need for multiple tablets?
It reduces the number of ordering tablets, but most restaurants still need separate screens for KDS, pickup display, and delivery acceptance. Physical screen count rarely drops to one.

What is the difference between a KDS and a pickup screen?
A KDS shows the kitchen what to make and in what order. A pickup screen shows customers or runners which orders are ready. They serve different audiences and usually need to be in different locations.

Are locking tablet mounts necessary for back-of-house screens?
Not always. Back-of-house screens used only by staff can use standard adjustable mounts. Locking mounts are most important for customer-facing or high-traffic counter positions.
