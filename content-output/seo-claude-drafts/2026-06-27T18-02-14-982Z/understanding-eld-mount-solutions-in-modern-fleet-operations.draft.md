## What the FMCSA Actually Requires for ELD Placement

The FMCSA ELD mandate allows smartphones, tablets, and wireless devices to serve as ELDs as long as they meet technical specifications, remain fixed during commercial motor vehicle operation, and stay visible from the driver's normal seated position. "Fixed" does not mean permanently bolted to the truck. It means the device cannot be loose or hand-held while the vehicle is moving.

That distinction matters for fleet managers. A quality suction or locking mount satisfies the fixed requirement without requiring permanent modification to the vehicle. It also means company drivers who cannot drill into a leased or fleet-owned cab still have compliant options available to them.

Three practical questions follow from the regulation:

  
- Where in the cab can the tablet be mounted so it is visible without obstructing the windshield view?
  
- Does the mount hold position on rough roads and during hard braking?
  
- Can the device be charged while mounted, so the driver is not hunting for a cable mid-shift?

## The Real Costs of a Bad Mount Setup

Loose tablets create a cascade of small problems that add up across a fleet. Drivers reposition devices constantly, which means attention off the road. Cables get pinched or disconnected, leading to dead batteries and missed log entries. When a tablet falls and cracks a screen, the replacement cost and downtime hit the operation directly.

There is also the shared-driver problem. In fleets where multiple drivers rotate through the same truck, a mount that one driver adjusted for their sightline may be completely wrong for the next person. A mount with full 360-degree articulation and a locking position solves that without anyone needing tools at shift change.

Dashboard clutter compounds the issue. Many cabs already carry a GPS unit, a phone, and a dash cam alongside the ELD tablet. A mount that positions the tablet cleanly, with cable routing built in, reduces the tangle and keeps the driver's sightlines clear.

## Placement, Cable Routing, and the Details That Actually Matter

Choosing the right mount is only part of the job. Where you place it and how you route the charging cable determines whether the setup works in practice.

For windshield placement, position the tablet low enough that it does not obstruct the driver's forward sightline, but high enough to be readable without looking down sharply. The A-pillar area or the lower center of the windshield are common choices in semi cabs. Avoid placing the mount directly over vents, which causes temperature stress on the suction cup and the device itself.

Cable routing matters more than most buyers expect. A USB-C or hardwire charging cable that crosses the driver's field of motion becomes a hazard. Route cables along the edge of the dash or down the A-pillar and secure them with cable clips. If the mount does not have a built-in cable management channel, plan for this before the install.

For fleets with multiple devices in the cab, think about the full picture before mounting anything. An ELD tablet, a phone mount, and a GPS unit can coexist without clutter if the placement is planned. Stacking mounts on the same windshield section creates visual obstruction. Spreading them across the dash with intentional cable routing keeps the cab functional and compliant.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Where should an ELD be mounted in a semi truck?

The FMCSA requires the device to be fixed during operation and visible from the driver's normal seated position. In a semi cab, the lower windshield center, the dashboard face, or the A-pillar area are common compliant positions. The device should not obstruct the driver's forward view and should be reachable without leaning or stretching.

### Can a phone or tablet be used as an ELD?

Yes. The FMCSA allows smartphones and tablets to serve as ELDs if they meet the technical specifications of the mandate, run a registered ELD application, and are fixed and visible during operation. A proper mount is required to meet the fixed requirement.

### What does "fixed and visible" mean for ELD placement?

Fixed means the device is secured and cannot move freely during vehicle operation. It does not need to be permanently bolted to the truck. A quality suction or locking mount satisfies this requirement. Visible means the driver can read the screen from their normal seated driving position without repositioning or leaning.
